# FHR study: how Full House Reviewed works, and what a Step By Step Reviewed run can borrow

Written 2026-11-11 from the archive in `dddsite/fhr/` and the one existing SBSR post (`dddsite/sbsrposts.json`, shown by `dddsite/stepbystep_reviewed.html`). Numbers come from a full-text pass over all 193 reviews (282k words).

## 1. What the archive is (and is not)

- **Complete for episodes:** all 192 Full House episodes (S1 22, S2 22, S3 24, S4 26, S5 26, S6 24, S7 24, S8 24) plus the bonus "Unaired Pilot" entry. One HTML page per review; `fhr/index.json` holds filename, date, title, "Firsts" text and block count.
- **Missing:** the "Season N Reviewed" wrap-up posts, the final retrospective, the bonus week posts, and every reader comment. Screenshots are not local: each `<img>` hotlinks `fullhousereviewed.files.wordpress.com`, so they break if WordPress drops them.
- **Not your writing.** FHR is a third-party blog by an anonymous author (signs off "The Artist Formerly Known as Billy Superstar"). Use it as a model of structure and pacing only; do not lift its jokes or sentences into SBSR.

- One post a week, Fridays, almost without a miss, for nearly four years. Short gaps (2-4 weeks) between seasons.
- Length grew **gradually**: Season 1 reviews are terse plot recaps with a joke per beat; by Season 7-8 they are 2-3x longer with digressions, theories and callbacks.
- The longest posts are the "event" episodes: the big emotional one (S7 "The Last Dance", 3041 words, hyped by commenters for years) and the finale (3106 words, half of it a farewell letter).

## 3. Anatomy of one review

In order, every time:
1. **Title** `Season N, Episode M, "Title"` and the original post date.
2. **Pre-Credits Gag:** one or two sentences describing the cold open, then a screenshot. Added from S2E4 and kept to the end (150 of 170 eligible posts). When an episode has none, the slot gets a joke about its absence or a preface (S7E17).
3. **Chronological recap.** The spine of every post. Scene by scene, present tense, plain verbs ("comes home", "tells him that", "enters the scene", "meanwhile, back at the full house"). The reviewer never skips ahead or reorders.
4. **Screenshot, then commentary**, roughly every 100-150 words. Images carry no captions; the paragraph after them does the work. Montages get 3-4 images in a row.
5. **Asides inside the recap:** logic holes ("how did she get the horse home?"), continuity breaks ("they remodeled the basement 2 episodes ago"), production cheapness (stock sounds, bad effects, the same set), outside references (other sitcoms, real places, the cast's later lives).
6. **The ending beat:** the music comes on, the talk, the hug, the audience goes "aww". Reported every time, with a running tally.
7. **Firsts:** a comma list of debuts (characters, catchphrases, sets, recurring habits). Heavy early (18 of 23 S1 posts), thinning out (5 of 24 in S8) as there is less new to log. Also logs joke "firsts" ("Danny acts like a real dad").
8. **Occasional tail:** announcements (forum, ads, merch, Facebook, wrap parties), "Note:" lines about broken comments.

## 4. The voice

- **Deadpan recap plus one twist per beat.** Most sentences report what happens straight, then the last clause turns it ("and then Danny is deeply touched, all the way down to his butthole").
- **Literal-mindedness as the main engine.** Take the sitcom logic at face value and follow it: the 3-minute circus that "saved" the kids, the choir left behind in Oakland, where the dead grandfather's body is.
- **Profanity is steady and casual** ("fuck" lines per season: 30, 76, 122, 102, 131, 113, 110, 131). It ramps up after Season 1 and plateaus; it is texture, not the joke.
- **Fixed characterizations, played for years:** Joey as the unwanted mooch everyone secretly hates; Danny the neat freak with a homoerotic Joey subtext; Jesse as a greasy, shiftless self-promoter; Michelle as a selfish brat; Stephanie's "total disregard for others"; the house itself as a malevolent force ("the full house", lower case, 524 uses) that consumes people.
- **Naming gags that track the story.** "Kimmie Gibbler" is always full name (300+ times). "Rebecca Donaldson" is always full name until her wedding, where she is "fully consumed by the full house", and from then on she is only "Becky" (with "Man, I miss Rebecca Donaldson" callbacks). The name change is the joke about the character.
- **Running counters and tallies:** number of "musics" in one episode ("That's like 4 musics! There's no way that's not a record"), catchphrase watch (Duane's "whatever"), "moments that actually weren't that terrible" (about 5 in the whole series).
- **Hedges and shrugs as rhythm:** "Anyway," (408 uses) to get back to the recap, "I guess" (314) to accept nonsense, "for some reason" (86).
- **First person grows over time:** S1 is mostly recap; later seasons add personal asides (grew up near SF, never saw this episode, reader requests, how tired he is), and the persona becomes a character readers know.
- **Grudging credit, rarely.** When something works (Duane's Shakespeare payoff in the finale), he says so plainly and then undercuts it. This makes the hate believable.

## 5. Long-arc devices (what makes a full run feel like a story)

- **Cast turnover as plot.** Grandparents who vanish without explanation become a recurring complaint that pays off when one dies (Papouli). Characters who leave are tracked and remembered.
- **Callbacks to old episodes** ("the horse in the living room" in S7) reward long-time readers.
- **Running theories** stated once and then treated as canon (Danny and Joey's boat as a "gay love boat").
- **Reader loop.** Comments hype upcoming episodes, request things, and feed jokes back. FHR added a forum, ads, shirts, paintings, two wrap parties, a Reddit AMA.
- **Season wrap-ups** ("Season N Reviewed") between seasons; a planned bonus week and retrospective after the finale.
- **The ending.** The finale review recaps straight, then drops the act: thanks, the 4-year habit ("Thursday night at a little after 1 AM"), sign-off. The sincerity lands because it is the only time.

## 6. SBSR so far, compared with FHR

The one SBSR post (S1E1 "Pilot", dated 2026-01-19, by Big Juicy Doug, 4535 words, 30 local images in `dddsite/stepbystepreviewed/`):

- **Already has its own voice, and it is not FHR's.** Markdown section headers per scene, bold punchlines, acronym gags (SBSCU, GM-BTU, GM-BSBSCH), deliberate false facts played straight and then contradicted (Al "dies" of appendicitis and the review carries on), long digressions (Urkel as class enemy), a closing segment ("Best Patrika Darbo Moment"), a bonus bit (Brady Bunch Test, 75% Brady) and a one-line conclusion.
- **Already has FHR-style seeds:** the cast-turnover complaint ("you too shall be Patrika Darbo'd"), the hug-and-music ending, fixed character takes (JT and Mark as the worst sitcom kids, Frank as "a walking boner", Brendan as "a blank space"), the copy-paste joke about Brendan.
- **Length is the problem.** The pilot is 6x an FHR Season 1 post and longer than FHR's longest review. FHR grew into length over four years. At 4500 words a week for about 160 episodes, the run will stall long before Season 7. Pilots earn extra length; regular episodes should start nearer 1200-1800.
- **Emoji in the Brady Bunch Test** (check and cross marks). Your style rule says no emojis; replace with text or inline SVG if that segment returns.
- **Screenshots are local here** (good: FHR's hotlinked images are a warning).

## 7. What to carry into a simulated SBSR run

Keep from FHR:
- A fixed per-post skeleton (title, date, cold-open slot, chronological recap, image every 100-150 words, music/hug tally, a closing log like Firsts).
- Weekly pacing and gradual growth in length and self-reference.
- Characterizations fixed early and played for the whole run; naming gags that change when the story changes a character.
- Tallies across the run (for SBSR: characters "Patrika Darbo'd", hugs, Frank sex mentions, JT crimes, how Brady each episode is).
- Season wrap-ups, a reader loop, and one sincere finale.

Keep from SBSR's own voice:
- Headers per scene, acronym lore, false-fact bits, the closing segment, the bonus bit. Rotate the closing segment by season (Darbo only lasts one) rather than retiring it.

Step By Step arcs a run can plan around (from memory; verify against an episode guide before writing any post):
- 7 seasons, about 160 episodes, 1991-1998; ABC TGIF for six seasons, CBS for the last. A network switch is a natural "the empire falls" beat for the GM-BTU lore.
- Penny and Ivy gone after Season 1 (the first "Patrika Darbo'd").
- Cousin Cody (the van, Bill and Ted type) as the long-running wild card, and his exit.
- Rich (Dana's boyfriend) arriving and leaving; a baby (Lily) born, then aged up between seasons; Brendan quietly dropped; Jean-Luc arriving late in the run.
- Every one of these is a callback payoff in the FHR style. Exact episode numbers, titles and air dates must come from a real source, not from memory.

## 8. Decisions for the simulation (2026-11-11)

- Output: a season-by-season run plan (arcs, tallies, how length and voice grow, wrap-ups, finale) plus 2-3 full sample posts from different points in the run.
- Location: a separate drafts JSON beside `sbsrposts.json` that `stepbystep_reviewed.html` does not read. Nothing goes live by accident.
- Facts: look up a public episode guide (titles, air dates, synopses, cast) before writing; flag anything unconfirmed. No plots from memory.
