Welcome to the Dotburg Wiki

Welcome to the Dotburg wiki, the guide to the little pixel town that grows by itself while its window is open. It starts as one house in a field and can become a city. You do not build it and you do not rule it: you watch from the leader's seat. See Watching, not ruling.

Watching, not ruling

From the Dotburg Wiki, in Getting started
Watching, not ruling
Information
TypeThe way Dotburg is played
LeversNone

Dotburg has no levers on purpose. Nothing you click changes what happens in the town. The leader makes choices, the townsfolk make choices, and the weather and the dice do the rest.

What you learn is what reaches the seat. People tell the leader what they know or what they want the leader to hear. Some news arrives late, some never arrives, and some is wrong. A feared leader hears more good news than there is. See Hearsay.

The picture of the town is always true: you see every dot-man walk where they walk. What you cannot see is why. The words, the bars and the tips are where the gaps are.

When the leader dies, falls or loses a vote, the seat passes on, and you see the town through the next leader's eyes, with their own friends and their own blind spots.

The aim is a feeling of smallness: one town among many, in a world that barely notices it. Things happen, people pass, and the town goes on.

The town window

The big window on the left. Its title is the town's name and size. Under the title is the government band: the town's banner, its rule, the The leader with their traits and health, the treasury, and the Townsfolk bar, which shows how the leader believes the town feels (see Mood). Below that is the picture of the town itself. Short calls from the town crier rise over the picture and fade.

The circular arrow in the title bar starts a new region (see Mother town and charter). Dotburg asks first and backs the old town up.

The ledger

Under the town window. Its title gives the day, the season and the weather. It shows the dot-men in town by colour, the buildings and what comes next, Food and hunger, the sister towns and a Troubles row. The button in its title bar rolls it up.

The Messenger

On the right, if the window is wide enough. It is the leader's own Messenger account. See The Messenger.

Hovering

Rest the mouse on a building, a piece of Land, a bar or a sister to see more. Tips show words, not exact numbers, and they show what the leader knows, not the whole truth.

Time and saving

From the Dotburg Wiki, in Getting started
Time and saving
Night falls on a town
Information
Day and nightA few minutes
SeasonFour days
SavesEvery half minute and on closing

The town only lives while Dotburg is open. Close it and the town waits, then carries on exactly where it was left. If you would rather it kept going for a while after you close it, see Work offline.

A day and night takes a few minutes. A season lasts four days, and a year has four seasons. Dot-men age much faster than the seasons turn, so a leader can grow old within a few reigns.

The town saves itself every half minute and when you close the window. Some things in progress, such as a fire or an outbreak, do not survive a restart.

Work offline

From the Dotburg Wiki, in Getting started
Work offline
Information
MenuFile > Work offline
ChoicesOff, one hour, two hours
DefaultOff

File > Work offline in the Messenger sets what happens while Dotburg is closed.

  • Off (the default): the town waits.
  • Keep going for up to one or two hours: when you open Dotburg again, the town plays through that much of the time you were away, behind the signing-in page, before you see it.

Nothing runs while the window is really closed. The town catches up when it opens. Sounds and pop-up calls from the catch-up are skipped, but the chats, the crier and the chronicle keep everything that happened, and when the catch-up ends a Today window sums it up: every leader in that time and the other news.

Dotburg Today
Information
OpensAfter a catch-up
MenuView > Dotburg Today

When Dotburg has played through time you were away (see Work offline), a Today window opens once the catch-up is done, as the old Messenger opened one when you signed in.

It says how long the town kept going, then lists the leaders in that time: the one who led when you left and each who came after, with how they came in and the call that announced them. Under that is what else happened, from the lines the The chronicle thought worth writing down.

All leaders opens the roll of leaders. View > Dotburg Today shows the last one again.

Growth and tiers

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The town
Growth and tiers
A city in summer
Information
TiersHamlet, Village, Town, City
GrowsOn its own clock
Waits forFood to spare, free building sites

The town grows on its own clock. Every so often it gains a little more: a newcomer, a new building, an upgrade to an old one.

Tiers

TierWhat it brings
HamletHouses, a well, a shop, a park, a bakery, an inn, a church, a mill. A hamlet rule once enough grown dot-men live there.
VillageTwo-storey houses, the Fountain, street lamps, a The council of four, and a real rule chosen by the townsfolk.
TownThe Market day, the town hall, the School, row houses, a fire brigade.
CityThe Cathedral, the Hotel, the clock tower, and in time a Mother town and charter.

Each step up brings more street lamps and usually a festival.

When growth waits

  • Newcomers only arrive when there is food to spare (see Food and hunger).
  • New buildings and upgrades wait when every building site already has work going on (see Builders).

The ledger says what comes next. When a city is finished, it is chartered as a sister town and a new hamlet is founded.

Builders

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The town
Builders
Builders at work on new sites
Information
WhoFree grown-up dot-men
WorksBy day, in fair weather
DangerFalls from the scaffolding

Every new building, upgrade and rebuilt ruin starts as a building site with scaffolding. A site only advances while builders are at it.

Builders are ordinary grown-up dot-men who are free at the time. They walk to the nearest site that needs hands and work along its front; you can see a hammer glint over them. Able dot-men join more readily, idle ones less. Nobody builds after dark or in a storm, so a site opened in the evening waits for morning. Stone from rocks nearby makes the work quicker.

When too many sites are open at once, new building waits until one is finished. Scaffolding is not perfectly safe, and builders sometimes fall.

Hover a site to see how many builders are at work.

Land

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The town
Land
Information
KindsGrove, pond, rocks, marsh
Per townA few patches
Built onNever

Every new town has a few patches of land that nobody builds on. Which kinds a town gets leans by its landscape: pine country tends to groves, snow and desert to rocks, harbours to ponds and marsh. Tiles of one kind sometimes join into a bigger area, with a boardwalk, a forest path or a stony track between them.

KindGoodBad
GrovePleasant walks in spring and summer. Fireflies.A dry grove feeds a fire, and a big fire can run through it.
PondThe best bucket line for a burning neighbour. Ducks, sometimes a heron.Its ice is a danger in winter. Spills in a flood.
RocksStone close at hand makes building quicker.Young dot-men climb them, and sometimes fall.
MarshThe damp ground slows a fire.Marsh fever starts here; marsh streets flood first.

Small life is never harmed. Ducks, the cat, birds and fireflies may hide from rain or winter, but no famine, fire, war or illness ever touches them.

Hover a patch of land to see what everyone knows about it. Towns saved before land existed have none until a new region starts.

Day, night and seasons

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The town
Day, night and seasons
Winter snow on the roofs
Information
SeasonsSpring, summer, autumn, winter
Season lengthFour days

Day turns to night every few minutes. At night most dot-men sleep, though night owls stay out at the inn, the park and the fountain, and street lamps light the larger towns. Most lamps go out round midnight; the ones by the inn, the hall and other all-night buildings burn until dawn.

SeasonWhat it brings
SpringSowing and the first greens. Spring floods in wet towns.
SummerLong dry spells, more fires, walks in the grove, sweating sickness.
AutumnThe crop stands and is reaped. Falls from the hayricks.
WinterSnow, ice on the ponds, the winter cough, the cold.

Weather

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The town
Weather
A storm keeps the streets empty
Information
KindsClear, cloudy, rain, storm, fog, snow
Depends onLandscape and season

Each town's weather depends on its landscape and the season. Skies drift between clear, cloudy, rain, storms, fog and snow.

  • Rain puts fires out and makes them less likely. Long dry spells make them likelier and dry the groves.
  • Storms keep dot-men indoors and stop building and market day.
  • Rain or snow puts a festival off until it clears, and breaks one up if it turns.
  • Storms on soaked ground can cause a flood.
  • Lightning can strike a building, more often a tall one.
  • A wet sky makes grain in the barn spoil faster and helps some illnesses.

Festivals

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The town
Festivals
Bunting and dancing on the square
Information
Held forNew buildings, a new tier, harvest, victory, crowning, fairs
Put off byRain or snow
Called off byReal sickness or hunger

Festivals gather the town with bunting and dancing. They come when a public building is finished (round that building), when the town grows a size, after a good harvest (harvest home, in wheat bunting), after a victory, at a crowning and at fairs.

A festival waits for good weather and lapses if it never comes. A real outbreak of illness or real hunger calls a festival off; the crier says why.

Under a harsh rule the gatherings become rallies instead. A rally is ordered, so it goes on in any weather.

Market day

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The town
Market day
Stalls above the square
Information
NeedsA shop or market
More often underA merchant guild
Rarer underA harsh rule

Once the town has a Shop or a market, market day comes round every few days in good weather. Stalls go up above the square, two traders bring greens for the shelves, and a crowd gathers. The town earns fees and the mood lifts.

A merchant guild holds market day more often; a harsh rule less often, and with a banner over the stalls.

Market day draws pickpockets. The guards may catch one; otherwise the one robbed bears a grudge, and the crier only says that purses went missing. Feuding families quarrel more in the crowd.

From the Dotburg Wiki, in Buildings
House
A house
Information
TypeHome
Comes atHamlet
Upgrades toTwo-storey house (village), row houses (town)
KeepsA pantry of meals

Houses are where dot-men live. Each home keeps a pantry of meals, and its residents sleep there, fall sick there and, when their time comes, walk home to die there.

As the town grows, houses are rebuilt bigger: a two-storey house holds more, and row houses more again. Married couples share a home when there is room.

A home that buys far more than it needs is hoarding; it shows a small sack at its corner. See Food and hunger.

Dot-men

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The townsfolk
Dot-men
Information
Also calledDot-women; children are dotlets
Each hasA colour, a name, an age, a home, three traits

The townsfolk are dot-men and dot-women; their children are dotlets. Each one has a colour from the town they or their family came from, a name, an age, a home and three Traits.

Dot-men walk where they act: to work, to the counters, to a friend's house, to a fire with a bucket, to the scaffolding. You can see what they carry. You cannot see what they think.

The leader knows friends, family, foes and councillors well. A stranger is known only by repute, which is sometimes wrong. See Hearsay.

Friends and grudges

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The townsfolk
Friends and grudges
Information

FriendsFade unless kept up
GrudgesFade slowly, deep ones hardly

Dot-men who share a roof meet, and meetings make bonds. Warm traits make friends; a charmer makes friends with almost anyone. The cruel bully, the light-fingered pick pockets, the hot-headed fly at people, the jealous envy, two proud dot-men quarrel, and the cunning cheat at dice. The calm and the humble cool old grudges.

Friendships fade over a week or two unless they are kept up; loyal dot-men keep them longer. Grudges fade slowly, deep ones hardly at all, and a vengeful dot-man never forgets. When a grudge runs deep enough, the crier tells how it began.

What bonds lead to

  • Friends marry, and the hungry eat at a friend's table (see Food and hunger).
  • Grudges across two families can start a feud.
  • A grudge against the leader can end in an attempt on their life (see Leader health).
  • Friends of anyone arrested turn against the leader.

Friends marry. The closer the friendship, the likelier the match; sometimes a neighbour makes a match instead. Under a strict rule many weddings are arranged, and some couples do not look happy about it. A wedding across a feud ends the feud.

A married couple share a home and their bond never fades while both live. A widow or widower may marry again.

Each wedding brings a dotlet, who takes a parent's colour and family name and is born at home. Weddings slow when the town is unhappy or short of food. Under a curfew they come more often.

Under a Junta or a party state, dotlets march behind the patrolling guards as a youth brigade and are walked home at curfew.

Food is not just a number: it lives in places and has to be carried. The year

The fields are sown in spring, stand in autumn and are reaped over a few days. What is still standing when winter comes rots. Seed is kept back for next spring. A good harvest brings a festival; a failed one brings fear.

Hunger

Hungry dot-men walk to the nearest counter or their pantry. A counter serves one at a time, so lines form, and a bare counter keeps its line until it is restocked: the bread line. Dot-men who go hungry long enough grow weak and can starve. When a dot-man finds no food, a friend may feed them, and in hard times generous dot-men feed the street.

Panic

Panic follows what dot-men see: bare shelves, lines and bad news, not the true stores. Panicking families buy extra and hoard, which empties the shelves faster. Panic outlasts the shortage by a few days. A hoarding home shows a sack at its corner.

Feuds

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The townsfolk
Feuds
Information
BetweenTwo families
Started byA deep grudge, or an attempt on the leader
Ended byA hearing, a wedding, time

When a deep grudge runs between two families, they may fall into a feud. An attempt on the leader's life always starts one with the assassin's family. Children carry it on.

Feuding families quarrel in the streets, more on market day. How a quarrel ends depends on the rule:

  • Under guards and a harsh rule, someone is hauled away or both are fined.
  • Under a council, mayoralty, commune, elders or shrine keepers, the quarrel is often heard and the feud settled. A calm or humble party helps.
  • Elsewhere it may come to shouting or fists, and rarely a knife.

Feuding families make no friends with each other. A feud ends with a hearing, a wedding across it, time, or when one family has nobody left in town.

The leader is one of the townsfolk, chosen in the rule's own way: elected, inherited, appointed, or simply the one who rose. They keep their own name and traits, and after a day in office they earn an epithet from their nature, such as "the Honest". A proud leader always gets a statue.

The leader's traits shape the town: how happy it is, how often they arrest, how they care for the sick, how the treasury fares, how likely a plot, a revolt or a scandal is. They also shape what the leader hears (see Hearsay) and the colour of their Messenger.

A reign ends with death, an assassination, a coup, a lost election, a rule change or stepping down. The seat passes, and so does your view: the new leader has their own friends, enemies and blind spots. A former leader who stays in town keeps their title as "former". Every reign is kept on the roll of leaders.

Who sits

The rule decides how seats are filled. A monarchy favours friends and kin, a theocracy the pious, a guild the greedy and thrifty, a junta and party state the hard, and elders the eldest. A dot-man passed over or put out of the council may bear a grudge.

Why it matters

Councillors are where most of the leader's news comes from. A skilled councillor on good terms with the leader reports truly; a poor one, or one who resents the leader, misses or bends bad news. New councillors take a few days to find their feet. See Hearsay.

Each reason in a tip shows how much it weighs in words, who told it and how long ago.

Dread

The more the leader is feared, the less bad news reaches them. A harsh rule, or a cruel, proud or vengeful leader, makes people stay silent or soften what they say, and a well-wisher may report praise nobody sings. Good news grows in the telling. A humble, tolerant or merciful leader hears more. Friends tell it straight. A survived attempt on the leader's life makes people speak plainly for a while.

When the seat passes

A new leader starts knowing nothing and hears everything afresh, with their own blind spots.

From the Dotburg Wiki, in Rule
Guards and arrests
Information
Kept byHarsher rules
PatrolsJunta, party state

Harsher rules keep guards. Every guard on the picture is a real dot-man walking.

  • Patrols walk the streets under a junta or a party state.
  • An arrest stops the dot-man where they are; a guard walks over and leads them out of the west gate. Friends of the one taken turn against the leader.
  • When the counters are rationed, guards stand at their doors.
  • Curfews send everyone home after dark.
  • Under the harshest rules, guards break up street tables as unlawful gatherings.
  • Guards on market day may catch a pickpocket.

When a rule change makes patrols unwanted, they walk out of the gate.

Sister towns

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The region
Sister towns
Information
Each hasPeople, colours, a rule, a ruler, a treasury, feelings toward us
ShownIcons in the ledger, chats in the Messenger

The town is one of several in a region. Each sister town has its own people, colours, rule, ruler and treasury, and its own feelings toward this town. The ledger shows them as a strip of icons; hover one to see her regard for us and why, as the leader heard it.

Between towns

  • Dot-men move both ways: newcomers arrive, emigrants and exiles leave for a named sister.
  • Envoys, letters, fairs and caravans come and go. Caravans can bring grain when the town is short.
  • Friendly sisters send aid in a disaster, and we send aid to them.
  • Towns make pacts and quarrels. Similar rules get on; a gentle town and a harsh one do not.
  • Sister towns change rule, revolt, absorb one another or collapse.
  • Spies come, and the guards may catch one.

When a disaster or a war strikes a sister, her people may arrive here as refugees. Each sister has a chat in the Messenger. For war, see War.

A new region begins with the city our settlers came from: the mother town, which is warm toward us from the start.

When this town becomes a finished city, it is chartered: it becomes a sister town itself and a new hamlet is founded in a new landscape. The old city becomes the new hamlet's mother. You follow the new hamlet from its first house.

The circular arrow in the town window's title bar starts a whole new region instead. Dotburg asks first, backs up the old town and puts the old chronicle aside.

Information

Wars come more often when this town or its neighbours are harsh. Hamlets are never attacked.

Soldiers march out of the west gate. News comes from the front, a soldier may be lost, and raiders may set fire to the town. While the war lasts, the coffers drain, the mood darkens under a gentle rule, and envoys, fairs and caravans stop.

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The region
Plague and famine
Information
SpreadsTown to town across the region
Plague likelierWinter, wet skies
Famine likelierAfter a dry spell, in winter

Now and then a plague or a famine starts somewhere in the region and spreads from town to town for some days.

In this town a plague brings the grey plague. A famine makes everything dearer, spoils the stores and the standing crop, darkens the mood, and some dot-men die of hunger or leave.

Towns struck by a blight lose people and gold. Caravans come only from towns outside it, and less often. Friendly towns send aid.

Fires

From the Dotburg Wiki, in Events
Fires
A house on fire, with a bucket line
Information
Starts fromKitchens, ovens, lightning, firebugs, raiders
Fought byRain, wells, ponds, bucket lines, a brigade
LeavesA Ruin

A fire can start from a kitchen or an oven (likelier in summer, in dry spells, and at bakeries, inns and mills; never in rain), from lightning, from a firebug, or from a saboteur or raider.

A fire grows on what it burns and can jump to nearby buildings when it is fierce, and through a dry grove. Free dot-men run to help; at night the alarm wakes some sleepers. The brave come from further away; the craven stay home.

HelpsHinders

A pond or damp marsh nearbyWooden buildings
Floodwater at the door

Stone buildings resist fire, and wells and fountains never burn. A building that burns down becomes a ruin and is rebuilt after a while. The stores inside are lost, the treasury pays, and the mood dips. Under a harsh rule a saboteur is blamed and someone is arrested; under a commune or council there is a collection.

Floods

From the Dotburg Wiki, in Events
Floods
Floodwater in the low streets
Information
Caused byStorms on soaked ground, spring rain
Worse withPonds and marsh
LeavesRuins, sickness

When storms fall on soaked ground, water rises in the low streets and round the ponds and marshes, then slowly drains. Spring rain can flood towns with wet land. Ponds and marsh make floods come quicker; marsh makes them drain slower.

A flood slows fires whose doors are under water. At its height, a building or two in the water may wash away, and anyone asleep or sick inside may drown. The treasury and the mood suffer, and a poor town asks its sisters for aid.

For a few days after a flood, illness comes more often, especially from foul water.

Information

Every so often a comet hangs over the region for two nights, drifting across the sky at night when the sky is clear.

Whether it is an omen or a wonder depends on who lives in town. Where many are pious, gloomy or doomsayers, it is an ill omen and the mood dips; elsewhere it is a wonder and lifts it. Doomsayers cry doom far more often under it, and prophets come (see Wanderers).

The Messenger on the right is the leader's own account. It shows who the leader talks to and what they say. You can read every conversation, but you cannot reply: the typing box and the buttons do nothing, on purpose (see Watching, not ruling).

How people write

Everyone types in their own hand, shaped by their traits: some neatly, some in lowercase with no stops, some with text speak or SHOUTING. Grave news reads plainer. Each leader picks the Messenger's colours to suit them, so the window changes colour when the seat passes.

The chronicle

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The Messenger
The chronicle
Information
Kept byA dot-man, the chronicler
Filechronicle.txt in the save folder
SlantThe chronicler's own

The chronicle is the town's history: changes of size and rule, elections, leaders' deaths and coups, wars, harvests, famines and plagues, great fires, the first of each kind of public building, and dealings with the sister towns. Everyday deaths and small news stay out.

It is kept by a dot-man, the chronicler, in their own voice. A gloomy chronicler notes burials, a cheerful one weddings, a curious one strangers. A chronicler who likes the leader praises them and leaves out some arrests; one with a grudge sneers. When the chronicler dies, leaves or rises to lead, the pen passes on.

The Chronicle contact in the Messenger shows it, and it is kept as a plain text file, chronicle.txt, in the save folder. Starting a new region puts the old chronicle aside.

Friends and enemies

From the Dotburg Wiki, in The Messenger
Friends and enemies
Information
FriendsClose friends of the leader
BlockedThose who hate the leader, or whom the leader hates

The leader's own friends appear under Friends in the Messenger, and those who hate the leader, or whom the leader hates, under Blocked. The former leader appears too, while they live in town.

Friends write now and then about their own troubles, the leader's, the town's, or nothing much. An enemy says one last word and is blocked. The list changes as friendships fade and grudges grow.

When the seat passes, the old leader's personal chats go and the new leader signs in to their own list. How the old leader fell becomes a real bond: a coup leaves a grudge, a handover a friendship.
